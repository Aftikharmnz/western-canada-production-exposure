Western Canada Production Exposure — Documentation

Production Systems & Map Markers

Each dot on the map represents a production facility or project. Dot colour indicates the production system — the type of hydrocarbon produced or the facility classification. Dot size scales with production volume at the selected date.

ColourSystemDescription
In-situ BitumenBitumen extracted underground without mining, using thermal methods such as SAGD (Steam-Assisted Gravity Drainage) or CSS (Cyclic Steam Stimulation). Steam is injected to reduce viscosity; emulsified bitumen flows to a production well. Dominant method in the Athabasca oil sands.
Mined BitumenBitumen recovered by open-pit surface mining. Ore is excavated, mixed with hot water (Clark Hot Water Extraction), and the bitumen froth is upgraded or diluted for pipeline transport. Confined to deposits shallow enough for economic mining (generally <75 m depth).
Synthetic Crude Oil (SCO)Upgraded bitumen that has been processed in an upgrader (hydrocracking or coking) to produce a lighter, pipelineable crude that trades similarly to conventional light crude. Produced at integrated mining + upgrading facilities (e.g., Syncrude, Suncor Base Plant).
Conventional CrudeLight and medium crude oil produced from conventional reservoirs in Alberta, Saskatchewan, British Columbia, Manitoba, North Dakota, and Montana. Canadian well/facility volumes are sourced from Petrinex, BCER, and Manitoba public files; North Dakota well volumes come from NDIC monthly production reports; Montana Bakken/Three Forks well volumes come from Montana BOGC.
Heavy Crude (Conv.)Conventional heavy crude oil (typically <22° API) from Alberta and Saskatchewan. Not bitumen — produced from conventional reservoirs but with higher viscosity and density than light crude.
CondensateUltra-light hydrocarbon liquid (60–80° API) that condenses out of natural gas at surface conditions. Used heavily as a bitumen diluent (forming dilbit) for pipeline transport. Produced at gas wells and gas plants.
Pentanes Plus (C5+)The heaviest NGL fraction — pentane and heavier hydrocarbons that are liquid at atmospheric conditions. Blended into diluent streams or sold as a light naphtha feedstock. Reported separately from condensate in Petrinex.
Gas PlantA gas processing plant that extracts NGLs (propane and butane) from raw natural gas. Produces mix-grade (MX) propane and butane only — no on-site fractionation to sales-grade product. See Volume & Streams tab for details.
Integrated PlantA gas processing plant with on-site fractionation capability. Reports both MX (raw extract) and SP (sales-grade) volumes. The facility extracts NGLs from gas and separates them into spec-grade products in the same complex.
Spec Plant (Fractionator)A standalone fractionation plant. Receives raw NGL mix from pipeline or truck and fractionates it into individual products: propane spec (C3-SP) and butane spec (C4-SP). Reports SP activity only — no raw gas processing.

Volume Terminology & NGL Streams

Units

Production volumes are monthly daily averages in bbl/d or kbbl/d. Petrinex AB/SK, BCER, Manitoba and AER ST39 monthly cubic metres are converted using 6.28981077 bbl/m³ and divided by calendar days. AER ST53 publishes m³/day, so no further division by days applies. NDIC and Montana monthly barrels are divided by calendar days. Production contains official reported actuals only. Analyst workbook history and forecasts have been removed; official AER history begins in 2017.

The gross stream sum measures selected asset activity. Bitumen and its upgraded synthetic crude, and NGL mix and spec products, can represent different processing stages of the same material. Do not interpret their combined sum as net supply. Later months with incomplete source reporting are labelled partial coverage.

Activity Codes (Petrinex)

Petrinex tracks what happens to a product at a facility using activity codes. The two most relevant for NGL volumes are:

CodeNameWhat it means
PRODProductionRaw production at the wellhead. Used for oil and gas production volumes.
PROCProcessingVolume of NGL extracted from raw natural gas at a gas processing plant. This is the primary activity used for propane and butane volumes on this map — it captures the volume produced at the gas plant.
FRACFractionationVolume of NGL separated into individual product streams at a fractionation plant. Used by standalone fractionators and integrated plants for their spec-grade output.
DISPDispositionSales or transfer volumes. Used for tracking downstream movement, not production.

Important: propane and butane use PROC only in the current app. That is a plant extraction view, not a full market supply view. Pentanes Plus is intentionally different: it uses C5-SP PROC plus FRAC because that is the total definition requested for this app.

Mix vs. Spec (MX vs. SP)

GradeCodeWhat it isWhere it comes from
MixMXRaw, unfractionated product extracted from natural gas at a gas processing plant. Contains impurities and may be mixed with other NGL components. Not yet at sales / pipeline specification.Gas plants (orange dots) and integrated plants (purple dots) — PROC activity
SpecSPSales-grade product meeting pipeline or end-user specifications. Produced after fractionation — the process of separating the mixed NGL stream by boiling point into individual components.Spec plants (gold dots) and integrated plants (purple dots) — FRAC activity

In plain language: raw gas comes out of the ground mixed with propane, butane, and heavier liquids. A gas plant separates these liquids from the gas stream (PROC), producing propane mix and butane mix. A fractionator then takes that mix and distills it into pure propane spec and butane spec for sale.

Wildfire Layer

Fire Weather Index (FWI)

The Fire Weather Index is a numeric rating of fire intensity potential developed by the Canadian Forest Service. It combines six sub-indices based on temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and cumulative rainfall. FWI ranges from 0 to over 100; higher values indicate greater fire danger.

FWI RangeDashboard screening band
0 to <5Low
5 to <15Moderate
15 to <30High
30+Extreme

Optional CWFIS satellite hotspots are coloured by the dashboard's FWI screening bands. Official Alberta and BC feeds show reported incident locations, not surveyed fire perimeters. The proximity metric sums selected production within 200 km of visible reported fires; the nearer band is 75 km. These distances and the wind corridor are exploratory screening assumptions, not a fire-spread forecast or a prediction of production loss. Failed feeds show incomplete coverage.

Alberta Fire Status Codes (ABFP)

StatusColourMeaning
Out of ControlThe fire is spreading beyond defined control lines and available suppression resources are insufficient to contain the perimeter. Highest risk designation.
Being HeldThe fire is not currently expected to spread beyond its defined perimeter given available resources and weather conditions. Suppression is active and managing the boundary.
Under ControlThe fire perimeter is fully contained. The fire is no longer spreading; mop-up operations may continue to extinguish residual burning material.
CarryoverA fire from a previous fire season that was not fully extinguished (smouldering underground) and has re-ignited or is still being monitored. Common in peatlands.

StatusMeaning

AER Scheme Approvals

The AER Sites layer shows oil sands scheme approvals issued by the Alberta Energy Regulator (AER) under the Oil Sands Conservation Act. Each polygon represents an approved project area, not necessarily the current active extraction footprint.

In-situ Approval (teal polygon)

Approval for a thermal in-situ oil sands recovery scheme. The operator has regulatory approval to extract bitumen using subsurface thermal methods within the designated area. Common methods include:

  • SAGD (Steam-Assisted Gravity Drainage): horizontal well pairs — steam injected in upper well, bitumen drains to lower producer well.
  • CSS (Cyclic Steam Stimulation): single well alternates between steam injection, soak, and production phases.
  • THAI / VAPEX: experimental combustion and vapour-based methods.

Mineable Approval (amber polygon)

Approval for an open-pit surface mining oil sands scheme. The operator has AER approval to mine bitumen-bearing ore from the surface. Generally applies to deposits less than ~75 metres deep where in-situ methods are uneconomic. Mining produces large volumes of tailings that must be managed on-site.

ST102 Facilities

AER ST102 is the Facility List. In this app the ST102 markers are the subset of mapped oil-sands-related facility points from the AER Facility List Shapefile, useful for cross-referencing production volumes against physical facility locations and Petrinex facility IDs where available.

ℹ️ Scheme boundaries are regulatory approval polygons sourced from the AER open data portal. They may not exactly match the current operational footprint or the extent shown on satellite imagery.

Pipeline Layers

Pipelines shows AER Alberta feeder pipelines and pipeline installations from the local GeoJSON exports. Market Pipes opens a route picker for long-haul crude, diluent, HGL, and refined-product systems. Rail & Terminals adds main rail corridors, major industrial leads, crude-by-rail points, and bulk crude/refined-product terminals. A terminal marker does not imply rail service; each Canadian point identifies its available public transport modes.

The map opens in Topo mode. Use the Map: Dark / Topo switch above the lower-right zoom buttons to change the background for the current session. Dark mode keeps province, city, town, and smaller-place labels while muting most other basemap detail, and it uses brighter railway colours so infrastructure routes remain legible.

AER feeder layer

Official AER pipeline and installation publications are checked weekly. Mapped shapefile records are reconciled against the source tables and converted from NAD83 to WGS84 before publication. Feeder categories load as map tiles after zooming in. They retain operating and permitted lines in the selected substance classes; installation menus retain operating, permitted and suspended facilities. Records without published coordinates remain unmapped. Each segment retains its available operator, substance, licence, diameter, pressure and source identity.

Market access layer

The market access layer keeps named routes readable by default: Enbridge Mainline/Lakehead, Trans Mountain/Puget Sound, Keystone, Express/Platte, Southern Lights, Cochin, Line 9, Wascana, Westspur, Enbridge Bakken/North Dakota, and optional downstream Enbridge/CER systems. It now also offers hidden U.S. EIA national reference networks for crude trunks, petroleum products, and HGLs, plus the Imperial Sarnia Products, Sun-Canadian, and Valero Pipeline Saint-Laurent Canadian refined-product corridors. The three Canadian product routes are approximate intercity corridors sourced from current operator descriptions. EIA's national layers show major/trunk and selected intrastate systems, not every pipe. Yellow hub markers identify key terminals, refineries, interconnects, and route nodes.

Market hub tooltips and click-to-pin hub popups include current public crude, condensate, heavy, and super-heavy tariff rows where published by the carrier. Each row keeps the official currency, fixed and variable toll components where the tariff publishes them, additive charges, included charges, conditional charges, loss allowances, all-in values where the adders are numerically defined, per-barrel conversions for cubic-metre tariffs, effective date, status, document name, and a PDF page link.

Rail and terminal layer

The rail and terminal layer is lazy-loaded from public/data/railways.geojson. Rail linework is BTS/FRA NET=M main routes and NET=I major industrial leads for Canada and the United States; it intentionally omits comprehensive yard, siding, minor-lead, and private-spur track. About 110,000 source segments are retained but grouped into roughly 1,300 browser render features. The app imports every valid point returned by EIA's crude-by-rail and petroleum-product terminal services. It adds 75 reviewed Canadian facilities from Suncor, Imperial, Shell, Trans Mountain, Enbridge, Irving, Valero, Gibson, Torq, Keyera, HF Sinclair, and other public operator/NPRI sources. That Canadian inventory is a high-confidence current tranche, not an exhaustive government registry.

EIA's public petroleum-product terminal GIS service is an older snapshot (service data edit: 2020), so complete service-point coverage is not the same as a current all-U.S. operator roster. The inactive terminal category is off by default; it retains the Edmonton Rail Terminal because Pembina reports that facility was decommissioned in Q2 2024.

Market access lines are analytical context only. They are approximate and must not be used for construction, ground disturbance, or exact underground pipeline location checks.
